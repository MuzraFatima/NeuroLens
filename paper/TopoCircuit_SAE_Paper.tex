\author{Muzra Fatima}

\begin{abstract}
Feature absorption, where a specific sparse-autoencoder (SAE) latent takes over the direction of a more general one, has so far been measured only on single-parent hierarchies. We introduce MPAbs, which scores absorption separately for each of three simultaneous parents (first letter, WordNet category, case) of single-token nouns, and DR-SAE, an SAE that learns a frequency-ordered, acyclic parent graph between its latents and routes each child's activation to its ancestors. On a CPU-only budget we train seven SAEs (width 2{,}048) on 857{,}504 Pythia-70M layer-3 activations and evaluate them on 52 MPAbs cells. Against Matryoshka at matched effective $L_0$, DR-SAE shows a -34.0\% relative reduction (negative means an increase; 95\% CI -318.4\% to 59.1\%, one-sided Wilcoxon $p = 0.7$, $n = 52$ cells). On synthetic data with a known graph, DR-SAE recovers exact parent sets for only 0.13 of children (bar: 0.80) and its edges reproduce across seeds less often than a post-hoc baseline. In an exploratory analysis, DR-SAE leaves parent latents silent less often than Matryoshka, but a one-parent tree prior does so as well. We report all pre-registered outcomes, including the failed ones.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Sparse autoencoders decompose language-model activations into sparse, interpretable latents. Chanin et al.\ showed that the sparsity penalty rewards \emph{absorption}: a child latent (``bat'') can encode its parent's direction (``animal''), leaving holes in the parent latent. Matryoshka SAEs, tree-structured SAEs and AG-SAE reduce absorption, but every published score uses the first-letter task, in which each token has one parent. Real concepts have several. This work asks four questions: (RQ1) how absorption behaves with several parents and which parent goes first; (RQ2) whether learned DAG routing reduces it at equal sparsity; (RQ3) whether the learned edges are real and stable; (RQ4) whether lower absorption gives smaller circuits.

\section{Method}
\textbf{DR-SAE.} A BatchTopK encoder gives native codes $z$. A sparse non-negative parent matrix $A$ with at most $m = 8$ candidate parents per latent gives ancestor-closed codes $z_{\mathrm{eff}} = \sum_{h=0}^{H} A^h z$ ($H = 4$) and reconstruction $\hat x = R z_{\mathrm{eff}} + b$. Candidates are re-proposed every 100 steps from co-activation and decoder similarity, and may only point from a more-frequent to a less-frequent latent, so the graph is acyclic by construction. Loss: nested Matryoshka reconstruction over four prefix banks, $\lambda_A\lVert A\rVert_1$, a whitened orthogonality penalty between parent and child atoms, and AuxK.

\textbf{Baselines.} BatchTopK; Matryoshka BatchTopK; a tree prior (DR-SAE restricted to one parent); AG-SAE-lite (greedy, cycle-free, coverage-based parent induction on a trained Matryoshka SAE, then one realignment phase with fixed edges); Post-hoc DR (DR-SAE routing on a frozen Matryoshka encoder). All start from random init, see the same data for the same number of steps and use native $k = 32$; a second DR-SAE uses a smaller $k$ so that its \emph{effective} $L_0$ matches the baselines.

\textbf{MPAbs.} Single-token Pythia nouns whose lowercase and TitleCase forms are both one token, with the lexicographer file of their most frequent WordNet sense. Cells are letter $\times$ category groups with at least 20 tokens (52 cells, 1828 tokens), split 60/20/20 by word. A logistic probe per parent is kept if its validation balanced accuracy is at least 0.90 (7 of 26 kept). Parent $p$ is absorbed on token $t$ when its probe fires, the SAE's best-F1 latent for $p$ is silent, and another active latent contributes at least 40\% of the token's probe projection.

\textbf{Circuits.} Subject-verb agreement in three templates (simple, across a prepositional phrase, across a relative clause; 48 prompts each). The SAE is spliced in after layer 3 with its error term kept; latents are ranked by attribution patching and we report the number needed to recover 80\% of the logit difference.

Every method's strict MPAbs is below 3\%, a floor effect that leaves little room to separate them.

\textbf{Exploratory (added after results).} The parent false-negative rate (the parent's latent is silent where its probe fires) does separate methods: against Matryoshka, DR-SAE at matched effective $L_0$ shows a 37.3\% relative reduction (negative means an increase; 95\% CI 19.5\% to 53.9\%, one-sided Wilcoxon $p = 5.2e-05$, $n = 52$ cells), and the one-parent tree prior a 63.7\% relative reduction (negative means an increase; 95\% CI 49.5\% to 76.8\%, one-sided Wilcoxon $p = 1.3e-07$, $n = 52$ cells). Because the tree prior does at least as well, this gain does not depend on multiple parents.

\section{Limitations}
One seed on the language model, so no seed-level intervals; a 70M-parameter model, 2{,}048 latents and under one million tokens; 52 MPAbs cells rather than the planned 100; single-layer circuits on three templates; baselines reimplemented from paper descriptions. An earlier version of this project reported results that were not produced by running the models; they were withdrawn (see \texttt{docs/DEVIATIONS.md}).

\section{Conclusion}
MPAbs makes multi-parent absorption measurable on a small model on a laptop. Routing parents through a learned frequency-ordered graph changes how often they are absorbed, as reported above, but at this scale the learned graph itself neither matches a known ground truth nor reproduces across seeds, which is the clearest target for future work.
