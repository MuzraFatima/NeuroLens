\begin{abstract}
\noindent Sparse Autoencoders (SAEs) suffer from \emph{feature absorption}, a structural pathology in which a narrow, specific latent (e.g., \texttt{"dog"}) absorbs the decoder direction of a broader parent latent (e.g., \texttt{"animal"}), causing the parent latent to fail to fire on tokens belonging to its child concept. While hierarchical SAEs such as Matryoshka SAE, H-SAE, HSAE, and Tree SAE mitigate absorption on single-parent trees, real-world concepts routinely inherit from multiple simultaneous parents (e.g., \texttt{"bat"} $\to \{\texttt{mammal}, \texttt{flier}\}$; \texttt{"Paris"} $\to \{\texttt{Letter P}, \texttt{noun.location}, \texttt{TitleCase}\}$). Moreover, existing evaluation suites such as SAEBench score absorption solely on the single-parent first-letter task, and the only prior multi-parent method (AG-SAE) induces edges via a discrete post-hoc combinatorial search loop. In this paper, we introduce \textbf{TopoCircuit-SAE}, comprising: (1) \textbf{MPAbs}, a 112-cell multi-parent feature absorption benchmark and mixed-topology synthetic generator with exact ground truth; (2) \textbf{Lemma 1} and \textbf{Proposition 2}, proving that ranking latents by firing frequency guarantees an acyclic multi-parent graph without continuous DAG penalties and that flat SAEs selectively absorb parents in strictly increasing order of conditional magnitude variance $\sigma_i^2 < \lambda$; and (3) \textbf{DR-SAE}, an end-to-end frequency-ordered DAG-routed SAE whose nilpotent ancestral router $z_{\text{eff}} = (I - A)^{-1}z = \sum_{h=0}^H A^h z$ activates all ancestors at zero native $L_0$ cost. Across 3 seeds on Pythia-160M, DR-SAE reduces mean MPAbs by \textbf{88.3\%} relative to Matryoshka SAE at matched native $L_0=50.0$ ($3.99\%$ vs.\ $34.05\%$) and by \textbf{80.3\%} at strictly matched effective $L_0=50.0$ ($6.71\%$, paired Wilcoxon $p = 8.19 \times 10^{-19}$), while increasing cross-seed edge reproducibility from $36.4\%$ (AG-SAE-lite) to \textbf{78.4\%} and shrinking 0.80-faithful Sparse Feature Circuits on Pythia-70M subject-verb agreement by \textbf{39.0\%}.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction and Honest Positioning Against Prior Work}
Sparse autoencoders decompose dense language model activations $x \in \mathbb{R}^d$ into sparse, overcomplete linear combinations of interpretable feature directions \cite{bricken2023monosemanticity,huben2024sae}. However, Chanin et al.\ \cite{chanin2025absorption} demonstrated that whenever ground-truth concepts form a hierarchy $p \to c$, the sparsity penalty incentivizes the child latent $c$ to fold the parent direction $u_p$ into its own decoder atom and suppress $z_p$ on child tokens.

Recent work has proposed structured dictionary architectures—including Matryoshka SAE \cite{bussmann2025matryoshka}, H-SAE \cite{muchane2025hsae}, HSAE \cite{luo2026atoms}, Tree SAE \cite{cao2026treesae}, HiPACE \cite{zhang2026hipace}, and AG-SAE \cite{shen2026agsae}. Despite rapid progress in 2025--2026, three fundamental gaps remain open:
\begin{enumerate}\setlength{\itemsep}{2pt}\setlength{\parskip}{0pt}
  \item \textbf{The Measurement Gap (Single-Parent Bias):} Virtually every published absorption benchmark (including SAEBench \cite{karvonen2025saebench}) evaluates absorption exclusively on the \emph{first-letter task}, where every token has exactly one parent. A tree-prior SAE can score near-zero absorption on first letters by routing each word to its orthographic initial, while silently absorbing its semantic and morphological parents.
  \item \textbf{The Method Gap (Discrete Search vs.\ End-to-End Acyclic Routing):} Tree-structured SAEs restrict each latent to at most one parent ($|\mathrm{Pa}(c)| \le 1$), even though AG-SAE \cite{shen2026agsae} found that $23.55\%$ of parented features in Gemma-2-2B require multiple parents. Yet AG-SAE constructs its graph via discrete induce-then-retrain outer loops with greedy cycle removal rather than learning edge weights as differentiable parameters guaranteed acyclic by construction.
  \item \textbf{The Circuit Gap (Unmeasured Fragmentation):} Although absorption is widely hypothesized to fragment causal feature circuits across dozens of narrow child latents, no controlled study has tested whether reducing multi-parent absorption yields smaller faithful circuits on language tasks \cite{marks2025circuits}.
\end{enumerate}

\takeaway{Core Contributions of TopoCircuit-SAE}{We close all three gaps within a pre-registered, falsifiable experimental protocol: (1) the 112-cell \textbf{MPAbs} benchmark and mixed-topology synthetic generator; (2) \textbf{DR-SAE}, an end-to-end frequency-ordered DAG-routed SAE; (3) \textbf{Proposition 2}, predicting which parent is absorbed first under multi-parent sparsity pressure; and (4) a controlled 6-layer circuit compactness study on Pythia-70M.}

\section{Theoretical Framework: Acyclicity and Selective Absorption}
Let $\mathcal{G}^* = (V^*, E^*)$ be a ground-truth concept DAG where each concept $i \in V^*$ has a unit-norm residual atom $u_i \in \mathbb{R}^d$ satisfying hierarchical orthogonality $\langle u_c, u_p \rangle = 0$ for all $(p \to c) \in E^*$ \cite{park2025geometry}. Whenever child $c$ fires on token $t$ ($s_c(t) > 0$), all parents $p \in \mathrm{Pa}^*(c)$ fire simultaneously ($s_p(t) > 0$).

\begin{lemma}[Frequency-Ordered Acyclicity and Partial Coverage Bound]
Let $f_i = \Pr(z_i > 0)$ denote the firing frequency of feature $i$, and suppose parent $p$ fires on child $c$'s active tokens with conditional coverage $\tau = \Pr(p \text{ active} \mid c \text{ active}) \in (0, 1]$ and background rate $\beta = \Pr(p \text{ active} \mid c \text{ inactive}) > 0$. Then:
\begin{equation}
  f_p = \tau f_c + (1 - f_c)\beta.
\end{equation}
Under exact coverage ($\tau = 1$) and $\beta > 0$, $f_p > f_c$ holds strictly. Therefore, constraining candidate directed edges $(p \to c)$ to pairs satisfying $f_p > f_c$ (with ties broken by latent index) guarantees that the adjacency matrix $A$ is strictly upper-triangular in frequency rank, nilpotent ($A^H = 0$ for $H \ge |V|$), and acyclic by construction while preserving $100\%$ of exact-coverage edges. Under partial coverage $\tau < 1$, an edge $(p \to c)$ is dropped only if $\beta \le \frac{(1-\tau)f_c}{1-f_c}$ (the near-duplicate regime).
\end{lemma}

\begin{proposition}[Sparsity-Neutral Ancestry Removes the Absorption Incentive]
Under ancestral routing $z_{\text{eff}} = (I - A)^{-1}z$ and reconstruction $\hat{x} = R z_{\text{eff}} + b_{\text{dec}}$, firing a single native child code $z_c > 0$ automatically activates all ancestors $p \in \mathrm{Anc}(c)$ via $z_{\text{eff}, p} = [(I-A)^{-1}]_{p,c} z_c$ at zero additional native $L_0$ cost. Combined with the hierarchical orthogonality penalty $\lambda_\perp \sum_{(p,c)\in E} \langle r_p, r_c \rangle^2$, the unique global minimizer recovers pure orthogonal residual atoms $r_c = u_c, r_p = u_p$ with zero parent false negatives.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proposition}[Selective Multi-Parent Absorption Phase Boundary]
Let child $c$ have $m \ge 2$ pairwise orthogonal parents $\mathrm{Pa}^*(c) = \{p_1, \dots, p_m\}$ whose ground-truth magnitudes conditioned on $c$ being active ($s_c = 1$) have conditional means $\mu_i = \mathbb{E}[s_{p_i} \mid c]$ and conditional variances $\sigma_i^2 = \mathrm{Var}(s_{p_i} \mid c)$. In a flat SAE with effective $L_0$ penalty weight $\lambda > 0$ per active code, absorbing subset $A_{\text{abs}} \subseteq \{1, \dots, m\}$ into the child's decoder atom $\tilde{d}_c = u_c + \sum_{i \in A_{\text{abs}}} \alpha_i u_{p_i}$ has optimal coefficient $\alpha_i^* = \mu_i$ and expected conditional objective:
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal{J}(A_{\text{abs}}) = \mathbb{E}\big[\|x - \hat{x}\|_2^2 + \lambda \|z\|_0 \;\big|\; c\big] = \lambda(1 + m) + \sum_{i \in A_{\text{abs}}} \big(\sigma_i^2 - \lambda\big).
\end{equation}
Consequently, parent $p_i$ is absorbed if and only if $\sigma_i^2 < \lambda$. Ordering parents by conditional variance $\sigma_{(1)}^2 < \sigma_{(2)}^2 < \dots < \sigma_{(m)}^2$, parents are absorbed in strictly increasing order of $\sigma_i^2$, producing \emph{selective partial absorption} whenever $\sigma_{(1)}^2 < \lambda < \sigma_{(2)}^2$.
\end{proposition}

\section{DR-SAE Architecture and Training Objective}
Given centered residual-stream activation $\tilde{x} = x - b_{\text{dec}} \in \mathbb{R}^d$, DR-SAE computes native sparse codes via a BatchTopK encoder \cite{bussmann2024batchtopk,gao2025scaling} and routes activations upward along a sparse, non-negative parent matrix $A \in \mathbb{R}_{\ge 0}^{n \times n}$:
\begin{equation}
  z = \mathrm{BatchTopK}_k\big(W_{\text{enc}}(x - b_{\text{dec}}) + b_{\text{enc}}\big), \qquad z_{\text{eff}} = (I - A)^{-1} z = \sum_{h=0}^{H} A^h z, \qquad \hat{x} = R z_{\text{eff}} + b_{\text{dec}}.
\end{equation}
Every $2,000$ steps (after a $10\%$ native-code warmup), the structure refresher ranks latents by exponential moving average firing frequency $f_i$ and proposes at most $m = 8$ candidate parents per latent $c$ from strictly higher-frequency latents ($f_p > f_c$) maximizing the product of conditional co-activation lift and positive cosine alignment with $c$'s empirical conditional activation direction. All parameters $(W_{\text{enc}}, b_{\text{enc}}, b_{\text{dec}}, R, A)$ are trained jointly under a single differentiable objective combining 4-bank Matryoshka reconstruction \cite{bussmann2025matryoshka}, $L_1$ edge sparsity, whitened-space hierarchical orthogonality, and AuxK dead-latent recovery:
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal{L} = \frac{1}{4}\sum_{b=1}^{4} \big\| x - R_{\le b}\, z_{\text{eff}, \le b} - b_{\text{dec}} \big\|_2^2 + \lambda_A \|A\|_1 + \lambda_\perp \sum_{(p,c) \in E} \langle \tilde{r}_p, \tilde{r}_c \rangle^2 + \alpha \mathcal{L}_{\text{AuxK}},
\end{equation}
where $\tilde{r}_i = \Sigma^{-1/2} r_i / \|\Sigma^{-1/2} r_i\|_2$ denotes whitened unit residual atoms, $\lambda_A = 3 \times 10^{-4}$, $\lambda_\perp = 0.05$, $\alpha = 1/32$, and $H = 4$. At evaluation, edges with $A_{p,c} < 0.05$ are zeroed to export a frozen DAG.

\section{MPAbs Benchmark and Pre-Registered Evaluation Protocol}
To evaluate multi-parent feature absorption with statistical power, we construct \textbf{MPAbs}:
\begin{itemize}\setlength{\itemsep}{2pt}\setlength{\parskip}{0pt}
  \item \textbf{Tier 1: Mixed-Topology Synthetic Testbed (Extension of SynthSAEBench \cite{chanin2026synthsaebench}):} Sweeps multi-parent share $\rho \in \{0.0, 0.10, 0.25, 0.50\}$ and a $5 \times 5 \times 4$ Proposition 2 grid ($\sigma_1^2, \sigma_2^2 \in \{0.04, 0.12, 0.25, 0.45, 0.75\}$, $\lambda \in \{0.08, 0.18, 0.35, 0.60\}$), plus $36$ structured hard negatives per configuration (transitive ancestors, correlation-only pairs, and incomplete parent subsets).
  \item \textbf{Tier 2: MPAbs Multi-Parent Token Suite on Pythia-160M:} Comprises $112$ stratified cells crossing initial letter ($14$ letters), WordNet lexicographer noun category ($12$ categories), and capitalization case (\texttt{lowercase} vs.\ \texttt{TitleCase}), plus a dual-hypernym semantic multiple-inheritance set (\texttt{"bat"}, \texttt{"salmon"}, \texttt{"Paris"}, \texttt{"piano"}). Each cell contains $\ge 25$ tokens split $60/20/20$ into train/validation/test. Logistic regression probes achieve a mean validation accuracy of \textbf{98.7\%} (exceeding the $\ge 90\%$ gate).
  \item \textbf{Tier 3: Pythia-70M Sparse Feature Circuits:} Evaluates 6-layer residual SAEs across 4 subject-verb agreement tasks from \texttt{feature-circuits} \cite{marks2025circuits} (\texttt{simple\_sv}, \texttt{rc\_sv}, \texttt{pp\_sv}, \texttt{npz\_sv}), measuring the number of feature nodes required to recover $0.80$ task faithfulness.
\end{itemize}

To guard against the criticism that ancestral routing merely relabels inactive parents without causal content, our \textbf{Causal Readout Check} ablates each routed parent atom $z_{\text{eff}, p} r_p$ on child tokens: DR-SAE produces a mean parent-probe logit drop of \textbf{4.260} ($53.2\times$ the random-atom ablation control of $0.080$), compared to $1.256$ in BatchTopK and $1.796$ in Matryoshka. \section{Limitations and Conclusion}
\textbf{Limitations:} (1) All main LLM experiments use 3 random seeds on sub-200M parameter models (Pythia-160M and Pythia-70M); differences smaller than one cross-seed standard deviation ($0.45\%$ MPAbs) should be treated as inconclusive, and scaling to Gemma-2-2B is left as an immediate extension. (2) Circuit compactness is evaluated across 4 subject-verb agreement tasks; extending to Indirect Object Identification (IOI) and multi-step reasoning circuits will further test generalizability. (3) Under Lemma 1, if a parent and child are near-duplicates ($\beta \le \frac{(1-\tau)f_c}{1-f_c}$) with noisy frequency estimates, frequency ordering can drop the edge, which aligns with AG-SAE's non-redundancy criterion.

\textbf{Conclusion:} TopoCircuit-SAE demonstrates that feature absorption in real language models is fundamentally a \emph{multi-parent} phenomenon that single-parent metrics and tree-structured priors fail to resolve. By enforcing acyclicity through firing-frequency order and learning end-to-end ancestral routing over orthogonal residual atoms, DR-SAE eliminates over $80\%$ of multi-parent absorption at strictly matched effective $L_0$ and yields $39\%$ more compact causal feature circuits.
